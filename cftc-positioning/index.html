Treasury futures: hedge funds' large shorts are mostly the "basis trade" (short futures, long the cash bond, to earn the small price gap). They are not straight bets on higher yields, so read rates positioning with care.
How to read this page
  • Net position is speculators' long contracts minus their short contracts. The small figure under it is that net position as a share of all open contracts in the market, so markets of different sizes can be compared.
  • 3-year rank compares this week's net position with every week of the last three years. "Most short in 3 years" means no week was shorter. When a trade is this crowded, a surprise can force many traders out at once and the move can be sharp. It is not a signal on its own.
  • All speculators is the CFTC's "non-commercial" group, the figure news wires quote. Hedge funds is narrower: leveraged funds for financial futures, managed money for commodities. The two can disagree, because "non-commercial" also includes asset managers.
  • FX futures are priced against the dollar: long yen means betting the yen rises, which is USD/JPY falling.
  • Positions are taken on Tuesday and published on Friday, so they are three days old when they come out.
Data: CFTC Commitments of Traders, futures only (Legacy report for all speculators; Traders in Financial Futures and Disaggregated reports for hedge funds). Positions as of each Tuesday, published Friday 15:30 New York (20:30 London). Not investment advice.
