How the numbers are worked out
  1. What the gauges are. Each one is an index built from option prices. Options are insurance against price moves, so when traders fear a big move they pay more for them, and the index rises. The number is the move traders expect over the next month, quoted as a yearly figure. VIX 20 means the S&P 500 is expected to move about 20% over a year.
  2. Expected daily move. To turn the yearly figure into a day, divide by 15.87 (the square root of 252 trading days). VIX 20 → about ±1.3% a day. For MOVE the result is in basis points of Treasury yield, not %.
  3. Where it sits in its year. We take every daily close of the past 12 months (about 252 days) and count how many were lower than today. 90 of 100 lower = "higher than 90% of the past year". That is the percentile, and it sets the colour: under 25 calm, 25 to 75 mid-range, 75 to 90 elevated, above 90 stressed.
  4. The range bar. Left end = lowest close of the past year, right end = highest. The white mark is today, the small grey arrow is one week ago.
  5. Currencies are different. There is no free feed of currency option prices, so for now these three gauges use realised volatility: how much the pair actually moved over the last 21 trading days (standard deviation of daily % changes × 15.87). It looks backwards, while the others look forwards. A paid feed (CME CVOL) would swap in implied FX vol later.
  6. Market stress today. The simple average of the percentiles of the option-based gauges (stocks, bonds, commodities). The currency gauges are left out because they measure past moves, not option prices, so mixing them in would blur the reading.
