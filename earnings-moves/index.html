How the numbers are worked out

Options implied move. The price of buying both the call and the put at the strike nearest the share price (a straddle), for the first options expiry after the results, divided by the share price. If the shares move by more than this in either direction, the straddle buyer makes money; by less, the seller does. It is taken from closing option prices before the report. It is only shown once the expiry is 5 trading days away or less: further out, most of the straddle's price is ordinary day-to-day movement rather than the results. When the expiry is a day or two after the report, the figure also covers those extra days, so it reads slightly high; the page says when.

Past moves. From the last close before the results to the first close after them. Results out before the US open are measured over that day; results after the close are measured over the next day. Report dates and timing come from Nasdaq's earnings calendar, going back about 3 years. Where Nasdaq doesn't say whether an older report came before the open or after the close, the timing the company used for its other reports is applied.

Past average. The average size of the last 8 moves, ignoring direction.

Past moves vs implied. The line of dots shows each of the last 8 moves after results by size, ignoring direction, against today's options implied move (the shaded bar). Dots past the end of the bar were bigger moves than the options now imply; the count under it says how many.

The options market started being saved on 18 Nov 2026, so the options implied move before older reports isn't available. This page shows what markets price, not a forecast.

Data: options from Cboe (delayed), earnings dates from Nasdaq, past report dates and daily closes from Nasdaq, market values from TradingView. Updated each weekday after the US close.
